# Hull, water, and rider dynamics

The reference was `../race64/fnport`: `physics.ts` (`contactSpringWave`, heading-projected water damping), `speedParams.ts` and `velocityAccel.ts` (low-speed contact torque), and `playerStateUpdate.ts` (secondary contact springs, rider inertia, and anchored hands/feet). The architecture document at `../race64/ghidra_c/PHYSICS_ARCHITECTURE.md` explains how those stages connect. No files in that repository were changed, and no ROM assets or tables are included here.

## Adaptation

Fast upward rebound receives additional water-contact damping: above vertical speed 4, a downward force of `8 * contactFraction * (vy - 4)` bleeds off launch energy. It acts only while the hull is wet, preserving gentle bobbing and the existing ballistic gravity once airborne. This shared hull rule reduces the tallest rough-water launches without changing the waves or imposing a height cap.

- **Hull:** a rigid pose with linear velocity and pitch, roll, and yaw rates. Six keel contacts sample water independently. Submersion and point velocity relative to moving water produce a clamped spring/damper force along the surface normal. Lever arms turn those forces into pitch/roll torques. Gravity and force integration replace the old surface-height snap. Downward acceleration is 22 world units/s²; the stronger pull shortens wave launches while retaining ballistic motion and spring-based landings.
- **Steering:** a torque response with damping builds angular velocity rather than immediately rotating the pose. A small idle maneuvering torque permits turning with no throttle. Authority rises through low speeds and falls again toward boost speed. Braking helps tight turns. The water-contact banking assist uses a progressive torque gain of `10 + 20 * abs(steer)`, giving roughly 13° of bank at half steering and 26° at full steering at cruising speed on flat water. Steering torque tapers between 24° and 31° of inward bank to keep the hull from tipping onto its side as the inside rail loses contact. This assist fades to zero at rest and without water contact; the torso retains zero sideways tilt. Propulsion and lateral grip require water contact; airborne motion retains momentum.
- **Rider:** the pelvis has independent damped translation springs; softer vertical suspension absorbs landings through a deeper crouch. The torso has zero sideways roll, including during impacts; the retained rider roll fields stay zero. One pitch spring preserves bounded forward acceleration lean and hull-pitch impact reactions. The pose uses the full lateral spring offset (previously scaled to 65%) so hips and chest shift together into turns and ease back to center without changing rider weight feedback into hull physics. The upper-body frame is transformed back into hull space with the inverse hull rotation, so combined pitch/roll does not tilt the torso or helmet with the ski. Shoulder anchors use that same frame. Elbows and knees use two fixed .55-unit segments. Iterative pelvis reach constraints reserve joint bend while keeping hands on the bars and feet on the deck; limb segments do not stretch. Weight shift still contributes bounded torque back into the hull. This follows the reference's independently upright upper-body frame and secondary contact idea with an analytic, compact solver.
- **Water feedback:** moving, wet hulls emit up to 12 short-lived traveling wake packets. CPU contact sampling and GPU water displacement use the same wake equation. This lets a wake displace another craft rather than being only a spray effect. Water geometry is finer near the player.

The base tune halves the original steering authority. The player now has three times that base idle authority (0.63 radians/second), with extra assistance fading smoothly to zero by speed 8. AI and cruising steering keep the base tune. Wave amplitude uses a shared `WAVE_SCALE = 2.4` (50% taller), with `WAVE_SPACING = 1.35` giving approximately 26% shorter wavelengths. Six layers combine long crossing swells, rolling wave trains, short chop, and wind ripples. Smooth spatial envelopes create calmer and rougher patches, while traveling amplitude groups vary the surface over time. The layer table generates the GPU equations and drives CPU sampling, so hull forces follow the rendered wave patterns. Boat wakes retain their separate amplitudes. Acceleration checks use flat water to isolate engine and steering behavior; full race checks exercise the actual swells.

Every rider builds speed levels 1–5 through correct checkpoints. Each step adds 8% to jet thrust and normal/boost speed caps. Opponents retain their fixed 15% base pace increase, multiplied by the same earned bonus; their driving target speeds follow both multipliers. Misses reset only the earned bonus, and later correct passes rebuild it. Missed checkpoint crossings remove the bonus immediately and clamp excess horizontal speed to that rider’s base cap while preserving direction and vertical motion. Existing Space boost remains independent. Restart resets the level and miss count. Each missed checkpoint is consumed, and five total misses end the player’s race.

## Collision interactions

The flat main island's land boundary sits close to the average waterline of its sloping sand mesh. This allows skimming beside the beach edge before shore contact pushes the craft back and resets its speed level. The steeper green island retains its original shoreline collision boundary.

The rider suspension extends toward a standing pose while airborne and compresses under upward hull acceleration. Gravity shared during free flight adds no relative rider load. The pelvis follows a small forward arc on extension so hands can stay on the grips. A damped steering pole lifts from -7° to +14° while its crossbar stays fixed in yaw; its pose supplies both the rendered crossbar and the hand constraints. Handle pitch is drawn with the current rider pose. The rider preview includes a real jump/landing cycle and a repeating wave-load check.

One horizontal circle at each craft's center handles racer and buoy contacts through a shared helper. Racer overlaps receive equal position corrections and equal/opposite horizontal impulses with low restitution; tangential velocity remains intact, and separating contacts receive no added impulse. Impacts update velocity and derived speed without directly changing yaw, roll, or rider springs. Vertical separation lets airborne craft clear another hull. Finished craft do not collide.

Each visible buoy, including both start markers, stays at its fixed horizontal position with a height-dependent collision radius. Collisions move the craft fully clear of the buoy and reduce its incoming normal velocity. Wave sampling directly drives buoy height and lean; buoy recoil, velocity, tilt velocity, and mooring springs are removed. Per-racer cooldowns limit repeated impact effects. All hull contacts resolve before checkpoint crossing tests, so the actual post-impact path determines whether a buoy was passed correctly.

## Step order

Physics uses fixed 1/60-second steps. Rendering runs only after a physics tick is due, targeting 60 Hz even on high-refresh displays. Frames draw current craft, rider, buoy, and water state directly; camera and effects advance by the elapsed fixed steps. Slow frames can process multiple steps before drawing once. There is no interpolation delay or stored previous pose. Display refresh rates that are not multiples of 60 can produce uneven frame spacing.

The race remains at 60 fixed ticks per second; each hull/rider tick uses four semi-implicit substeps (240 Hz). Each substep samples current contact points and surface velocity, accumulates buoyancy and torque, applies steering/propulsion/drag, integrates the hull, and advances the rider spring. Race collision and checkpoint handling follow. New wake packets become available to all craft on the next tick, avoiding racer-update-order feedback. Restart clears angular momentum and rider spring energy; there is no automatic recovery. Pausing freezes the whole state.

## Deliberate approximations

This is a compact physically motivated arcade model, not a full fluid simulation or ROM reproduction. The hull is rigid instead of a 12-particle constraint mesh. The rider uses three translation springs, one angular pitch spring, and analytic fixed-length limbs instead of the original 19-contact solver. Idle torque, active banking/trim, angle limits, speed caps, and the normalized units are game assists. Wakes are bounded analytical disturbances rather than a propagating water-grid solver; they do not reflect from shores. Rider attachment travel is limited, and there is no ragdoll or capsize state.

The limits and force parameters are named in `src/boat-physics.js`. Per-racer `contactPoints`, `contactFraction`, `waterForce`, `landingImpact`, `acceleration`, angular rates, and `rider` offsets provide diagnostics through the development harness. Tests cover rest equilibrium, stationary steering, sloped water, flight, landing transfer, anchored limb endpoints, rider load feedback, wake expiration, timestep consistency, restart, five-miss loss, and full AI races.

The close-up fixture at `test/rider.html` exercises held banking, combined rocking, and landing impulses, with separate torso sideways-roll, forward-lean, lateral-shift, and WebGL diagnostics. Additional tests cover combined hull rotations at multiple headings, zero sideways torso tilt, pitch recovery, lateral recentering, and fixed-length, bent limbs with anchored hands and feet throughout extreme poses.

## Shallow-water rendering

An analytical sand shelf follows the island shorelines and slopes down to a deep floor. The seabed and seeded seagrass are rendered before the translucent water. A depth-only water pass selects the nearest crest; the color pass blends depth- and view-dependent water opacity over the underwater scene. Animated caustic shading and underwater color absorption remain procedural. These scenery meshes do not change hull forces or add underwater collisions.

## Packed race layouts

The editable course designs generate encoded checkpoint and starting-grid data before AI lines are generated. Runtime coordinates use quarter-unit precision and headings use .001-radian precision; checkpoint widths and passing sides stay exact. Development and production use the same decoded geometry. Lighting follows consumed checkpoint count, eased between targets; lighting no longer reads the craft’s angular position.
