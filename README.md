# Sunwake Rush

A procedural, low-poly jet-ski racer: three islands, three laps, and three AI opponents. All meshes, colors, waves, effects, and audio are generated in code. No runtime dependencies, textures, fonts, or audio files are downloaded.

The title screen contains only the game title, four course names, and START. Title, pause, loss, and results reuse one menu panel, with a shared Start/Resume button and Courses action. One centered dark panel and a shared button style serve all menu states. The UI palette stays the same in day and night scenes; narrow screens wrap the HUD and short screens scroll the menu. In-race branding, the map header/caption, and the pause button are removed. The countdown displays only numbers and GO. The UI uses one generic sans-serif font and rounded short hex colors; procedural world colors remain unchanged. Four AI riders continuously race the selected course behind the menu, with that course's checkpoint buoys. Selecting a course resets this shared race and clears its trails. Start resets the same race to a clean countdown with zero progress and normal finish/loss rules; returning to Courses starts a fresh preview. A fixed panoramic camera keeps the rocky-island lighthouse on the right. The preview loops continuously. All four preview riders share the opponents’ look-ahead controller. Lighting follows the selected course and sound starts with the game; neither has a toggle.

## Play

Requires Node.js 20+. Install the pinned build dependencies once:

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:5179 and choose a ride:

- **Main island loop:** clockwise around the main island and through the channel, with 10 wider checkpoints and simple passing lines.
- **Main island reverse:** counterclockwise with a varied shoreline route, 16 narrower checkpoints, and alternating slalom lines.
- **Rocky island loop:** the extended 18-checkpoint route out around the rocky island and back, with taller crossing waves in the exposed sea.
- **Sunrise reef loop:** 18 checkpoints around the smaller beach island and out past the reef to the lighthouse, weaving through four staggered outcrops on the outer side of the smaller island, with expert opponents. The sun rises from predawn to daylight over three laps.

Use WASD to drive and Escape to pause. The pause menu offers **Resume** and **Courses**. There is no restart shortcut or retry button. Return to Courses and press Start to begin a new race. No keyboard hints appear in the game UI. Race modes have three AI opponents and three laps. Pass the highlighted buoys in order on the side shown by the colored arrow; five total missed buoys end the race. There is no automatic recovery or teleporting.

Each course has its own automatic lighting: the main loop stays in daylight, reverse gradually changes from sunset to dusk across the race, the rocky loop stays at night, and the reef loop progresses from predawn through sunrise to daylight. Lighting targets now follow the number of consumed checkpoints across three laps, including missed checkpoints, and ease smoothly between targets. Moving around a buoy cannot rewind the sky; the start line stays at the opening light and the final line reaches the ending light. Pausing holds lighting transitions. Starting from the course menu resets race progress. Navigate using the visible checkpoint buoys; the minimap is removed.

Water reveals a sandy seabed and swaying seagrass through depth- and view-dependent transparency. Animated caustics and underwater color absorption are restored, with a depth prepass to select the nearest wave crest. Depth-based surface colors, reflections, waves, and simplified shoreline foam remain. The lighthouse has a plain white tapered shaft, stands on the rocky island, and sweeps a three-dimensional, seven-band rainbow light volume across the surrounding sea, without a projected lighting pass on the water or shore. Six procedural rock outcrops extend toward it from behind the smaller beach island, with a gap for the original offshore route. Four more outcrops form a slalom east of the beach, opposite the main island. The rocks retain shoreline collisions. Offshore chop grows smoothly between the sandy island beach and the rocky island; hull physics and rendered water share the same wave parameters. The rainbow start arch is removed; checkpoint buoys remain. Side spray and collision bursts are removed; rear spray and trailing foam remain. Rear spray and trailing foam switch directly to full rainbow colors at buoy-earned speed level 5/5. Particle wakes remain six scattered flat foam triangles per emission, white below level 5; ripple arcs and rainbow ribbons are disabled. Missing a buoy resets the level. Existing trails fade naturally. Wakes are visual foam and spray only: they do not deform the wave surface or apply forces to boats and buoys.


Large background rock silhouettes reuse enlarged copies of the first reef rock mesh, including its smaller stones, far offshore beyond the playable islands. These visual copies add no collision landforms. Light local haze gives way to a longer distance fade, with fog colors following the sky through daylight, sunset, and night. The extended ocean and view distance keep the distant scenery visible. Sky, reflections, and haze share one elevation curve between blended horizon and upper-sky colors. Water normals reuse the center height with two forward samples, retaining all six wave layers and the same rendered heights and hull physics.

Seagulls are removed. The sun and moon occupy world directions: turning or pitching the camera reveals different sky, while the sun and moon also travel with the reverse-course sunset or reef sunrise. Stars are removed. Water highlights follow that same light direction. Lens flare is removed. Sky lighting uses shaders without image assets. Palm trees retain seven fronds each, simplified to one triangle per frond. The beach pavilion is removed.

The chase camera smoothly follows a fixed position 13 units behind the craft and 7.25 units above sea level, with a steady horizon and no hull bobbing or jump tracking. It looks 13 units ahead. Idle pivots retain the camera's heading; movement above speed 1, including sideways drift, smoothly recenters the view without a turn-rate cap. Physics and rendering run at 60 Hz. Each rendered frame uses the current craft, rider, buoy, and water state, with the camera and effects advancing on the same clock.

Soft circular blob shadows are shaded directly onto the water beneath all four craft. Their size and strength stay fixed during jumps; they follow the waves without heading data, extra quads, or blending passes.

Opponents drive faster on reverse and faster again on the rocky course, with more precise lines, quicker steering decisions, and faster straight-line targets. All opponents retain their fixed 15% base pace increase and now earn the same buoy speed levels as the player: +8% per clean pass up to level 5, reset to level 1 on any miss. They follow a smooth line through the buoy corridors, use the next two waypoints to anticipate the approaching turn, and brake early if their momentum would miss the next buoy. The build compiles the existing planner into compact per-rider corridor offsets; production decodes only the next two points. Shoreline clearance is checked by the planner; live steering retains buoy-crossing and drift correction without rescanning land or calculating second-corner braking. Run `npm run courses` after editing a route to regenerate packed checkpoints/starting grids first, then AI line data. `npm run build` also regenerates both in that order. Development and production both use the decoded layouts; editable splines and course diagnostics remain available only in development. Planned segments keep clearance from islands and reef rocks. Their driving target speeds follow the earned bonus, and level 5 produces rainbow spray. Occasional natural misses are allowed; no misses are forced.

Craft use one center circle for collisions with buoys and other racers. One shared collision response separates overlapping bodies and changes horizontal velocity, treating buoys as immovable and retaining sound feedback. They do not directly kick yaw, roll, or rider springs. Buoys stay fixed horizontally and follow wave height and slope; jumping clear avoids the body collision. Checkpoint locations remain anchored.

Each race event plays one short sine tone with a distinct pitch: countdown, start, splash, correct/missed buoy, lap, win/loss, impact, and reaching maximum speed level. Splash tones have a fixed pitch, volume, and duration, with the existing cooldown suppressing contact chatter. The player engine, race cues and moving-water rush are synthesized. A looping filtered-noise wash rises smoothly with player speed and fades out outside active racing. Shore surf, bird calls and rival engines remain removed.

All buoys share one uploaded mesh. A shader supplies red or cyan body/arrow colors; start/finish arrows remain black, model reflection gives each arrow its passing direction, and the next checkpoint is self-lit. Course changes reuse the same buffer. Rider elbows and knees use fixed .55-unit segments, with pelvis reach constraints that preserve joint bends; hands stay on the grips, feet stay on the deck, and rider springs, upright torso balance, turns, and landing compression remain. The steering pole follows the smoothed vertical suspension directly, retaining its travel limits without a separate spring. The pose computes relative torso rotation directly, projects the pelvis into the hand/foot reach limits, and solves each elbow and knee analytically. Rear droplets and trailing foam share one emitter loop and one flat shrinking triangle shape. They retain separate motion and lifetimes: droplets rise and fall under gravity while foam follows the water surface. Ordinary and maximum-level rainbow colors remain.

Larger wave crests carry broken whitecaps, with a narrow wash at the shoreline. The animated beach-wave bands are removed. Smooth random foam texture breaks up whitecaps and shoreline wash with one animated sample. Foam is generated in the water shader, follows the wave surface, and retains its distance fade and sunset and night colors. Sky, reflected sky, and distance fog share the same atmosphere palette.

The player starts at speed level 1. Each correctly passed buoy raises the level by one, up to 5, adding 8% engine power and top speed per step (32% maximum). Passing the expected buoy on the wrong side immediately resets the level to 1, and advances to the next checkpoint. Starting a new race resets the chain and miss count. The race HUD uses shared tiles for position, time, missed buoys, numeric speed level, and speed. The level is shown as a number out of five, without bars. Tiles wrap on narrow screens. The live leaderboard is omitted; final results show only finish times or “ON COURSE” in each craft’s color, ordered by placing, with no rank numbers, rider names, or color squares. The player’s row stays bold, beneath a single “Race complete” heading. A five-miss loss still says “Race over.”

Speed bonuses apply automatically as you pass buoys; there is no boost button or rechargeable resource. At level 1, the speed cap is 120 on the HUD, rising to about 158 at level 5; actual speed depends on turning drag and water contact. You can pivot slowly without throttle. Steering strengthens as you get moving, then broadens at high speed. Hold S while turning to brake into a tighter corner.

## Build and check

```sh
npm test
npm run build
npm run check:size
npm run preview
```

`npm run build:fast` uses the current compact shader/code variant for quick playtesting, Roadroller level 1 with a 32 MB memory cap, and 15 Zopfli iterations. `npm run build` first searches all 15 variants with and without that packing, then refines the three smallest packed variants using Roadroller level 2 with a 64 MB cap and Zopfli at both 15 and 100 iterations. It retains all earlier results and ships the smallest complete ZIP. The stronger release search takes longer to build.

The production build is a self-contained `dist/index.html`, also packaged as `dist/sunwake-rush.zip`. Extract the ZIP and open `index.html` directly to play offline. The build reports ZIP size without enforcing the eventual 13,312-byte limit.

Source modules stay readable. The build minifies JavaScript, CSS, HTML, and shader text, omitting optional HTML tags that browsers reconstruct; shortens an explicit list of internal properties and shader interface names; and excludes physics diagnostics used only by development fixtures. Production candidates also shorten shader-local names, try frequency-based name allocation and reduced JavaScript inlining, inline WebGL constants, and encode race states, event types, and lighting modes numerically. Redundant course defaults and checkpoint channel metadata are omitted from production; development fixtures retain their defaults and diagnostics. It also tries coordinated class/ID renaming across HTML, CSS, and DOM references in JavaScript. It also tries evaluating shader templates during the build and shortening their numeric literals, checking that the generated programs match development. It compares final ZIP sizes for every candidate and packages the smallest using standard ZIP Deflate with Zopfli, fixed metadata, and a round-trip integrity check. Compression compares zlib level 9 and Zopfli, retaining the smallest result even when more iterations make an archive larger. No system `zip` command is needed. The npm packages run only during the build. Roadroller adds a self-contained decoder to the inline game script; it restores and evaluates the JavaScript at startup without downloading anything. The standard decoder has a 32 MB memory cap (about 18 MB estimated working memory); stronger release candidates allow 64 MB (about 37 MB estimated working memory for the current game). Decoder variables remain scoped to avoid overwriting named DOM globals. Packing happens after HTML minification, and the build verifies that the decoded JavaScript has the same syntax tree before writing the ZIP. `dist/size-report.json` records candidate sizes, CSS bytes, the final archive size, Roadroller savings against the best unpacked ZIP from the same build, stronger-search savings, selected packing level and memory cap, estimated decoder memory, and the selected identifier map for development fixtures. The identifier map is excluded from the ZIP.

The build strips page descriptions/theme metadata, unused checkpoint diagnostics, and the spray debug tag. Level descriptions are removed from the source. Course names are retained to generate the five menu buttons; direct click handlers select their courses, and a selected class supplies the highlight. No data-course, aria-pressed, or extra label wrappers are needed. Favicon markup and ARIA labels are removed. No icon is required to run the game, although a browser may independently look for favicon.ico.

HTML retains the standards-mode doctype, with a UTF-8 byte-order mark instead of a charset meta tag. Mobile viewport metadata is removed; the game targets desktop browsers. The document title, explicit html tag/language attribute, group/status roles, and initial counter/countdown/result-heading text are removed; JavaScript fills those values before showing them. The visible game heading remains. HUD tiles use paragraphs with zero margins so production can omit their closing tags. The packager also omits safe attribute quotes and optional head/body tags. The browser tab has no explicit title, and the removed status roles no longer provide live announcements.

Earlier size passes reduced the ZIP from **44,374 to 23,052 bytes (48.1%)**, before the later feature cuts described above. CSS cleanup, UI simplification, short UI colors, and selector renaming reduced built CSS from 9,766 to 3,247 bytes. That pass still exceeded 13 KiB; the current build also applies the later reductions and Roadroller packing described above.

A subsequent model-generation pass reduced its working-tree ZIP from **24,458 to 24,134 bytes**, saving **324 bytes**. It simplifies riders, lighthouse details, wildlife, and marker markings while retaining scenery, rider motion, and all animal counts. The two model source files are 1,347 bytes shorter; geometry reductions are larger than the compressed-code savings.

## Structure

- `src/simulation.js`: fixed-step handling, AI, collisions, checkpoints, five-miss loss, and rankings.
- `src/course-codes.js`: generated layouts with quarter-unit positions and .001-radian headings; checkpoint widths/sides remain exact. All four grids and 62 checkpoints are retained.
- `src/racing-lines.js`: generated course/rider offsets from the decoded layouts; the original planner remains available in development.
- `src/interactions.js`: circle collisions, momentum transfer, wave-following anchored buoys, and collision splash events.
- `src/boat-physics.js`: six-point hull buoyancy, angular dynamics, rider springs, and anchored limb constraints.
- `src/course.js`: fixed course, shoreline, and shared wave function.
- `src/mesh.js` and `src/renderer.js`: procedural geometry with a shared grid for islands, spheres, and cones; custom WebGL renderer, water shader, camera, wakes, and spray. Distant silhouettes and decorative shoreline boulders use stretched spheres; collidable islands and reef rocks use uneven elliptical rings.

Hull decks and undersides share one edge loop, retaining their outline with four additional triangles per craft. Meshes send face vectors to the vertex shader for normalization. The small bow cone is built directly, upright and .55 units tall.
- `src/audio.js`: synthesized player engine, water, and race cues.
- `src/main.js`, `index.html`, and `style.css`: input, race screens, and HUD.
- `scripts/`: dependency-free development server, production minification, and deterministic single-file ZIP packaging.

Desktop keyboard controls and WebGL are required. Audio starts after the player starts a race. Losing focus pauses the race; resume with Escape or the on-screen button. There is no custom graphics error panel or reload flow. Shader/context diagnostics remain development-only and are stripped from production. No multiplayer or competition submission is included.

## Browser checks

After building, `/test/production.html` checks the actual minified package through its public UI: all four courses, driving, pause/resume, focus loss, removed restart shortcuts, WebGL, and zero subresource requests. The Node production test compares source and minified simulation/checkpoint/buoy outputs and generated geometry exactly. Test fixtures are excluded from the ZIP.

`/test/css.html` compares source and packed computed styles across 48 layouts, including six viewport sizes, day/night classes, HUD, menus, and pseudo-elements. It also checks horizontal overflow and short-screen menu scrolling. It accounts for equivalent zero-length serialization produced by minification. The fixture uses the build's identifier map without adding any lookup code to the game.

With the development server running, open `/test/browser.html` and click **Run keyboard + full race checks**. It tests the actual keyboard event handlers, pause, focus loss, returning to Courses, and drives a full race using digital steering. Keep that page active while it runs (about three minutes). The harness reports results and observed frame rate for the currently selected race route. **Check modes** verifies moving title riders, selected-course preview resets, the fixed title camera, selected layouts, clean four-rider starts, returning to previews, removed restart shortcuts, and WebGL state. **Check speed levels** runs quick deterministic checkpoint fixtures through the simulation and HUD to verify progression, the level cap, missed-buoy reset, rebuilding, five-miss loss, simulation freeze, removed restart shortcuts, and starting again through Courses. **Check collisions** drives a buoy impact and places a racer collision fixture to verify deflection, fixed buoy positions, wave-following tilt, retained wake particles, and WebGL rendering. Test files and the development state export are excluded from the production package.

**Check camera** runs a short keyboard-driven fixture covering title-to-race initialization, acceleration, braking turns, stopping, idle heading hold, and returning to the title camera. It also checks fixed sea-level height and WebGL health.

### Shader size and caustics comparison

The build also compares conservative shader-name reuse between separate functions and value-preserving GLSL numeric shortening without baking shader templates. Names within the same function remain distinct, and global/interface names remain protected. The smallest actual ZIP wins.

The caustics simplification trial was rejected for its grid appearance. The original caustics, seabed, seagrass and water transparency are restored; fish remain removed. Water foam uses one animated sample of the restored smooth random texture for whitecaps and narrow shoreline wash; incoming beach-wave bands are removed. Historical feature-cut previews are available at `/test/cut-study.html`; their packages are excluded from the production ZIP.

The selected layout, daylight, particle, and shader cuts reduced the ZIP from **14,905 to 14,070 bytes**, saving **835 bytes**. Subsequent shared-workspace cleanups bring the validated package to **14,006 bytes**, **694 bytes above 13 KiB**. Packed-course positions deviate from the editable designs by at most 0.175 world units; full AI races and buoy-clearance checks pass.

A behavior-preserving helper pass shares smoothing, water differences and speed limiting, specializes camera projection, and simplifies audio setup and small UI expressions. The frozen comparison saves **59 ZIP bytes with identical settings**, or **63 bytes with the full candidate search**. All 121 Node tests and 46 packed-browser checks pass; numerical camera, water, sampled race-state, and audio-operation comparisons match the immediate baseline.
